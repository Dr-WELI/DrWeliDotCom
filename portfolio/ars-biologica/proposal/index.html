1 · The proposition in one paragraph

A spatial ArtXScience work in which a visitor walks through the translation of a scientific finding into contemporary pop: first the research as material, then the translation made visible, then a timed screening of the finished film. The research is Shabarova et al. 2021 on Jiřická Pond, in which **dissolved nutrients return to predisturbance levels before microbial composition recovers**. One new pop song is written from that asymmetry, produced to release standard, and released publicly. The installation is the artwork. The film is a station inside it.

1a · What this continues from Kangaroo Time, and where it steps further

**Kangaroo Time is the best existing example of this practice**, and it is what the jury will watch. It came out of [my doctoral research](https://doi.org/10.1093/beheco/arad070), won the **AAAS Dance Your PhD Overall Winner 2024, and reached 238 media features across five continents**. What it proves is already relevant here: that a real finding can carry a pop song, that a cast drawn from several communities can be directed on location, and that the result travels.

This project continues three things from it and steps further on two.

1 · Science enters the sound itself.

Until now, science has entered my music through lyrics, concepts and movement, as in *Kangaroo
Time*. This will bring science into the sound itself: recordings become textures and starting
points for rhythm, melody and arrangement.

*(That sentence is from my artsACT application for bioacoustic curation, lodged 22 September 2026. It describes the next step in the practice, and this residency is where it meets a real research site.)* Here the material is the scientists' own: water, wind, sampling, the bench, the instruments, and the patterns they point at. Not illustration laid over a track. The track built from it.

2 · The film is made to music industry standards, not to a competition brief.

🔑 Kangaroo Time was made for the Dance Your PhD competition, and it carries what that brief asks for, including explanatory text on screen. A music video released into the industry does not explain itself.

So this film carries no explanatory text on screen. The science is legible through the installation, through the credits, and through the work itself.

⚠️ One deliberate exception, and it is the scientists' call. A captioned cut may serve the installation better than the clean release cut, because Station 2 is where the translation is meant to be readable. We would discuss with the scientists whether they want that version, and if so both cuts exist: the clean one for release, the annotated one for the room.

🔑 The direction of travel: Kangaroo Time made science legible to a broad audience through a competition format. This is more arty and more industry at once - further into the sci pop I am developing, and further into the standards a pop release is actually held to.

1b · The method, named

This project runs on a method I have developed and written up, rather than on inspiration.

🔑 The Interconnected Narrative is a four-step compositional method for translating real research into an artistic product: **theme → parallel mapping of the scientific and the socio-cultural narrative → polysemic elements → artistic product. Its theoretical contribution is the polysemic element**: one unit of the artwork legible inside both narratives at once, literally true in each.

⚠️ It is not metaphor. Metaphor describes one thing as another. A polysemic element is one form carrying two meanings that are both literally true. *(Method paper in preparation; the practice it belongs to, sci pop, is defined in Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.21387515.)*

Worked once already: in Kangaroo Time, made from [my doctoral research](https://doi.org/10.1093/beheco/arad070), the polysemic elements were "kangaroo time" and "mates" - each true in the behavioural-ecology reading and in the everyday Australian one.

Here the polysemic element is coming back. In the scientific narrative it is reassembly toward a predisturbance state, on unequal clocks. In the socio-cultural narrative it is a person returning to a place. One phrase, both literally true, which is why it can carry a pop chorus without flattening the science.

🔑 And that is the point of Station 2 in the installation. The connector shows the polysemic element being made: the reading, the mapping, and the decisions that came out of it.

4 · The installation, described properly

The visitor walks the translation. Three stations, one continuous path, continuous sound.

Station 1 · What we were given

Listening points carry the field recordings from the research sites, so a visitor hears the actual water, wind, sampling and bench sound before anything else. On mounted panels and screens: microscopy and field imagery from the scientists, the plots and the patterns they pointed at, and the printed finding in their own words.

🔑 Every item carries the name of the scientist who produced it. The credit is part of the display, not a line at the exit. A short printed legend separates observation, inference and artistic analogy, using the same three labels as the annotated cards from Phase 1.

This is the science, as science, credited.

Station 2 · What we did with it, and how

🔑 This station is the connector. It shows, item by item, how the paper became a song and a film.

Screens and listening points run side by side so the visitor can compare the source with what it became:

what is shownwhat it connects
The annotated cards, with the scientist's corrections visiblethe reading of the paper, and where they said an inference was actually an analogy
A pattern, then the sound it becamea plot or a field recording playing next to the rhythmic or melodic decision it produced
The lyric drafts, with the lines that changed after the 2027 public lab markedthe lyrical conversion of the finding, and what the local audience changed
The arrangement notesthe non-lyrical conversion: which motif is interrupted, which phase leads briefly, which voice reassembles late
The demo versions the scientists heard, with their responsesthe accuracy gate, in the artists' and the scientists' own words
The video script and concept boardshow the same finding produced the film's images and movement
Where an analogy was rejectedshown, with the reason

*This is the ArtXScience work itself, which is usually invisible. It is the reason the installation goes beyond a screen: the translation is the exhibit.*

Station 3 · The cinema session

A seated screening area. **The finished music video plays on a timed loop, a short session every few minutes**, the way a museum runs a film room, with proper sound. Between sessions the screen holds a still and the entry soundscape carries.

This is where the song arrives, as a song.

🔑 Why this shape. The call asks for a spatial work that goes beyond screen-based exploration. A film shown on panels is still a film. **A walk-through of how a scientific finding became a pop song is a spatial work in which the screen is one station.** It also puts the residency's actual subject, the collaboration, on display.

Deliberately low-risk: freestanding venue-approved structure, one projector, one sound system, no live water, no cultured organisms, no real-time data feed, no suspended rig, no wet flooring, no equipment operated by researchers.

Contingency: if the venue cannot host the full path, Stations 1 and 2 collapse into a single printed and sounded wall, and Station 3 stays. The logic survives at a smaller footprint.

It also stands alone: the song and film circulate online after the exhibition, with accessible captions and the same printed legend.

🔑 The scientists are co-thinkers, not final fact-checkers and not assumed performers. Any one of them can propose an unexpected sonic, visual or movement form, and Dr Dindhoria defines a contribution from his current research rather than being assigned a paper he did not author.

What the project offers them: an encounter between their work and music listeners and local residents, without asking them to endorse literal microbial characters or to perform in a video.

⚠️ No researcher is asked to make a new recording on demand. We request what already exists.

Conditions, written now rather than assumed: named credit on every piece that uses their material; explicit permission before any use, since scientific imagery and recordings belong to them and the institute; metadata recorded with every file (date, location, recordist, instrument, whether processed, permission to publish, image licence, consent for identifiable voices, agreed credit).

⚠️ We do not ask them to record specially on unpaid time, and we do not assume their work produces audible organism sounds. If the archive is not available, we record at permitted accessible sites and label that material accurately as an artistic layer, never as Jiřická data.
