Ars Biologica ArtXScience Residency 2027-28 · supporting material

Turning science into compelling art

I am Dr Weliton Menario Costa, known as WELI. I hold a PhD in behavioural ecology from the Australian National University, and I write pop songs, direct music films and stage spatial work out of published research. My own doctoral findings on kangaroo personality became a song, a film, a photographic series and a workshop. This page shows what that practice has produced, and what I propose to make with the freshwater scientists at the Biology Centre.

The practice, in time

My creative practice and my science have run together throughout. I sang and performed through my doctorate, from 2017 to 2021. The photography began in those same years, in the field: a photograph I took at the study site is published in my 2021 ANU thesis as Figure 2, carrying the credit line Image credits: Weliton Menario Costa (thesis PDF, p.15). The Photography Project followed in 2023, my first independent music releases the same year, and Kangaroo Time in 2024. The last three years made the work visible; the practice behind it runs back through the science that feeds it.

Kangaroo Time

My own research, as a pop song and a music film · 2023

I spent my doctorate following eastern grey kangaroos, measuring how individual personality, the social environment and maternal effects shape behaviour. The finding that stayed with me was variation: kangaroos in one mob differ from one another in stable, measurable ways. I wrote that into a pop song, and built the film around a Canberra cast whose dance styles differ as visibly as the animals did. I wrote and performed the song with producer Nicholas Moynihan; Nic Vevers directed photography and edit.

Watch the film

This film is the closest existing example of my practice. The proposal for Ars Biologica takes it two steps further. Kangaroo Time was made to a competition brief, so it carries explanatory text on screen. The freshwater film is made to international music-video standards with no explanatory text, unless the scientists want a captioned version for the installation, which we would discuss. And where science reached Kangaroo Time through lyrics, concept and movement, here the scientists' own field recordings enter the sound itself, as texture and as the starting point for rhythm and melody.

Cabra da Peste (G.O.A.T.)

A commissioned research-to-song, in Portuguese · 2024

The Brazilian Ethology Society invited me to open its 2024 annual meeting by speaking about creativity in ethology. I wrote a song for it. Cabra da Peste draws on my Honours research in Brazil on dominance among female goats, and my Honours supervisor appears in the track. The title is the reason the song works: in Brazilian Portuguese cabra names the animal and also names a person of real toughness and charisma, so one phrase is literally true about the goats and literally true about the women the song is addressed to. The research enters through popular culture and a human story, and the track stays a pop song throughout. It was produced with Jon Panic and released in December 2024.

The Photography Project

Staged photography from the same research · shot October 2023, released 2024

I am the creative director of this series. Concept and direction are mine; Tony Oliver and Reid Workman photographed it, with hair and make-up by Neha Suryavanshi, Shauna Priest and Anchal Goel, and technical support from Paris Smith-Davies and Leandro Eugenio de Souza. I cast it and set the brief for each model.

What it communicates. The series takes the same doctoral findings that produced Kangaroo Time and translates them into portraiture. Each model stands in for something the research measured: an axis of personality, a social role, a maternal effect. They are photographed in the open eucalypt country where the kangaroos were actually observed. A person in classical Indian dance costume and a person in drag are two extremes of human self-presentation, standing in the same paddock, in the same light, as the animals whose individual differences I spent four years measuring. You see the finding before anyone explains it to you. The images have since become the visual identity of my studio.

This is directly relevant to what I am proposing. It is evidence that I can run a crew of specialist artists toward a concept drawn from a scientific finding, and that the result reads without a caption. The residency asks me to do that again, with a different research group, in a different medium, in a different country.

How I work with the scientists

The method, step by step, with a worked example

  1. First, a conversation. Before any artistic decision is made. I sit down with the researchers and ask them to tell me about their research in their own words, and then ask the question that actually decides this project: of everything you have found, what would you most want a stranger to know? I am listening specifically for findings that speak to what this call asks for: climate emergence, sustainable agriculture, geoengineering, permaculture, water resource management, and the wider ecological effects that land on the people who live nearby. Out of that conversation we choose the research to work with, or the combination of findings, and I explain what I can and cannot do with each one. The scientists choose what their science is going to say. I choose how it is going to sound.
  2. Then I read the paper properly, and read it for the emotional connector. I go through the publication line by line looking for two things. The central problem, meaning the real-life pain the research exists to address. And the sentence where the finding itself carries a human feeling without needing a metaphor laid over it. Below is that read, done on a real paper from this Centre, so you can see exactly what I mean.

The worked example

Here is the read, on a paper by researchers at the Biology Centre. I am showing the mechanics, and the actual choice of paper belongs to step one.

Quote what you have

Release of dissolved nutrients from the pond is associated with inflow rates and community recovery, and returns to predisturbance levels before microbial compositions recover. Shabarova, Salcher, Porcal, Znachor, Nedoma, Grossart, Seďa, Hejzlar & Šimek (2021), Recovery of freshwater microbial communities after extreme rain events is mediated by cyclic succession, Nature Microbiology 6, p.479

The central problem underneath it

Extreme rain is arriving more often. After a flood, the chemistry of a water body can go back to looking like it did before, while the living community inside it has not finished coming back. A pond can measure as recovered and still not be recovered. For anyone managing water for a town, a fishpond or a farm, that gap between the number and the living thing is the real problem, and it is a problem people can feel.

Translate that poetically to a human emotion

Something has come back. I am not back yet.

Everyone has been in a room where the situation has returned to normal and they personally have not returned to normal. That is the connector. It is a human reading of the finding, and I would never present it as a scientific claim.

And that becomes the central emotional hook

It gives me a working title, Come Back Again, and one tension to write across all three outcomes at once.

In the songA chorus that asks for a return, over verses that sit in the distance between a measurement and a living thing. The hook comes out of the finding and stays answerable to it, while the delivery follows the song's logic instead of the paper's order.
In the music filmBodies that leave and come back changed. The long white sheet moving in open wind, which is the image I already hold for this work, reads as water, as a flood line, and as the thing that returns without returning the same.
In the installationThe visitor walks the same gap the paper describes: the sources first, then what was made from them, then the finished work. They arrive at the film having already heard the pond.

The example above is a demonstration of method. If the team judges that another paper gives the town and the science a more urgent starting point, we use that one, and a finding about a forest pond will never be presented as a finding about a managed fishpond.

  1. Then I ask them to send me material from their own study systems. Two kinds. Sound: recordings from the water and from anything associated with the organisms they study, including frequency and wave-pattern data where it exists. Image: photographs, video and figures that show the patterns they see in their microorganisms, their study populations and their ecosystems, along with the fieldwork itself. I ask for material that already exists and is already cleared, and nobody is asked to produce new recordings without agreement and resourcing.
  2. They send it. Then we talk about why they chose it. This second conversation is the one that does the work. What made you pick this recording? What am I looking at in this image, and what is remarkable about it to you? That is where I learn which patterns are meaningful and which are noise, and where a misreading gets caught before it reaches a lyric.
  3. Then I go away and immerse in it. I sit with the material, the research narrative and the central problem until the song starts. The writing runs on two tracks. Lyrics carry the emotional connector. The non-lyrical production carries the science directly: a real field recording used as timbre and texture, and documented patterns in the data used to inform rhythm, melody and groove. The visual material does the same job on the other side, informing the choreography, the script and the scenes of the music film, and setting the aesthetic of the installation.
  4. Three review points, and they hold a veto over the science. The scientists check the emotional premise and the early demo, then the source descriptions and credits, then the rough cut and captions. Anything misleading gets changed. My artistic decisions stay mine, and their science stays theirs.

The other artists, and the town

Who builds it, and who is in it

How I work with other artists

I am the principal artist, creative director, songwriter and performer. I conceive and approve the song, the script, the edit and the route through the room. Around that I hire specialists, because the standard I am aiming at is a commercial music release. I lead, and specialists build.

WhoWhat they do, and what stays mine
Music producerArrangement, recording, mix, master and separated stems, over three studio days. One of those days exists specifically to run the concept through the field recordings and the organisms' patterns, so the science reaches the arrangement as well as the words. A songwriting credit follows an actual agreed contribution.
Czech filmmakerDirector of photography and editor, working to a treatment I write and approve. Final edit approval is mine.
Movement leadA local movement professional. I prepare my own body for the shoot and author the central choreography. We talk first about how movement should flow through the piece and what this town would recognise, and they then adapt it to the local performers, rehearse them, and stage the shoot days with me.
Exhibition designer and AVA Czech specialist who prices, builds, tests, maintains and strikes the room safely, and writes the daily operating guide. They own the safety and the engineering; the spatial concept is mine.

Everyone is paid, credited, and holds their own permissions. The scientists advise on their research and their material, and are neither unpaid crew nor presumed co-authors of the song.

How I work with the community, who are not scientists

The performers in this film are people who live in the town. That is a decision about the work as much as about participation: a cast of residents puts the place the research is about inside the film about the research. I also want the videographer to be local wherever the host's networks can reach one, for the same reason.

Before any of that, I would sit down with a local movement professional and talk about how people here move, what movement language the town would recognise, and how that should flow through the choreography. I did exactly this on Kangaroo Time, where the cast's own dance practices shaped the film itself.

In 2027 I hold a public lab: I play two drafts, open the conversation, and invite anyone who wants to try the movement. I document at least two artistic decisions that the room changed or confirmed. In 2028 the finished work comes back to the same people, with the scientists and the performers in the room.

The installation, in the order you walk it

The artwork is the room. The film is one station inside it.

A short, accessible route between freestanding translucent fabric surfaces. Positioned speakers mean that walking changes what you hear, so the shape of the music is something you experience physically even when the film is not playing. Every label distinguishes what the scientists observed from what I made of it.

  1. 1 · The fieldworkImages of the work being done, from the research team and from my own time with them. You meet the people and the place before you meet any result.
  2. 2 · The publicationTheir paper, displayed and readable. The actual peer-reviewed science, credited, in the room.
  3. 3 · What they sent meThe bioacoustic recordings, the photographs, the video, the patterns. Playable and viewable as they arrived, before I touched them.
  4. 4 · How I workedDocumentation of the creative process: photographs, short videos, my writing, lyric drafts and the visual scores. Printed brochures that visitors keep and use, covering the process and what sci pop is, and the different ways research can enter music.
  5. 5 · The mini cinemaA timed screening of the finished music film, captioned, in a controlled listening space. You arrive here having already heard the pond it came from.
  6. 6 · What happened nextIf the song is released by the festival, the room ends with the release data, the media coverage and whatever impact it has had. The work carries on past the gallery door, and the last thing you see is the evidence of that.

There is no live water, no organism display and no sensor dependency. If the full-run AV hire proves unaffordable, the route keeps the positioned sound and the physical surfaces, the process material goes to print, and one screen alternates between process and film.

The travel, said plainly

One request, with the arithmetic behind it

The call sets the travel requirement at two to five trips to Budweis plus two trips to Ars Electronica, funded up to €3,000. From Sydney, only the bottom of that range is fundable, so that is what I am proposing.

I live in Sydney and hold an Italian passport, so I can work and travel in the EU. A return Sydney to Prague fare sits around €1,100 at the low end, so two crossings consume the allowance almost exactly once airport transfers, the Prague to Budweis rail and the Budweis to Linz rail are counted. A third crossing from Australia does not fit, and I will not budget as though it does.

So the version I am proposing is the minimum compliant reading of your own specification: two stays in Budweis, with both Ars Electronica visits made by rail from Budweis inside those stays. Everything the call requires still happens.

So I am asking for two stays: September 2027 and March 2028, the festival month. September replaces the first scheduled stay, and I would film while I am there, with the song already finished and mastered before I fly. That puts me in the country when the local performers, the scientists and the locations are all available to the same shoot, and it puts me back for the festival when the work goes up.

This needs the organisers' written agreement, because the call describes September as an optional second stay, and I am asking for it to stand in for the first. Everything before September runs remotely: the research conversations, the material exchange, the writing, the crew scoping and the preproduction.

One detail I would want confirmed early: the call covers one month of accommodation in České Budějovice from a separate budget, and which of the two stays that applies to changes the subsistence arithmetic above. I am raising all of this now so it is settled before delivery starts.
